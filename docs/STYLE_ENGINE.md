# Local style engine (experimental v0.4)

## What is implemented

The input is a sealed, human-reviewed set of English paragraphs from at least three independent papers, with one family reserved as holdout. Only development families contribute to the style profile. The server reads the private text on the user's computer, computes measurements, and returns aggregate values. It does not fine-tune, train, embed, or send the source paragraphs to an LLM. The manifest does not request excerpt permission for this aggregate-only workflow; there is no raw-example MCP tool.

For a section, each paper first produces a vector: median sentence words; sentences per paragraph; fractions of sentences with hedges, first-person pronouns, passive-like verbs, citation-like markers, semicolons, parentheses, questions, and fixed-vocabulary contrast/reason/limitation cues; rates of 16 fixed function words; and usage of a small fixed vocabulary of connectors and stance verbs. The component-wise median across independent paper families is the target. A component with large disagreement across papers does not yield a rewrite instruction. A section with fewer than two usable families falls back to the whole development set and reports that fallback. The new function-word rates are diagnostic and supplied as aggregate targets, not explicit commands to insert words. These fixed lexicons and regexes are only rough proxies; they do not infer argument structure, individual phrasing, rare word choices, or scientific reasoning.

`rewrite_plan` compares those targets with the input draft and orders at most four changes by normalized size. Its contract tells the host LLM to preserve assertions, comparisons, groups, directions, numbers, units, citations, negation and calibrated uncertainty, and to avoid copying source material. `assess_revision` reports a diagnostic difference before and after, text hashes, and lexical alarms. It always returns `needs_human_review`; it never certifies meaning or author likeness. The host application must explicitly invoke the tools, and the final displayed text must be checked against the returned revision hash for the assessment to apply to it.

## Verification and limits

- Synthetic tests check that local aggregation works when `model_excerpt_permission=no`, holdout text has no effect, no original passage appears in an MCP response, the rewrite plan responds to engineered stylistic differences, numeric mutations are flagged, and the official SDK can call the tools over stdio.
- The private four-PDF extraction smoke test produced enough provisional all-section candidates for the algorithm to run but too few independently supported candidates in individual sections. The rows have **not** been visually reviewed or confirmed as primarily written by the target author, so these measurements are not a valid style profile and are not served through MCP.
- Heuristics such as passive-voice detection and sentence splitting can misread scientific notation, abbreviations, tables, and OCR. Family-balanced averages can still track journal and topic differences. A lower diagnostic distance is not proof of human-recognizable authorship.
- Validate with confirmed writing contributions and blinded, same-content author ratings across sections, alongside independent fact checks. Keep evaluation papers untouched while iterating. See [evaluation protocol](EVALUATION.md).

## Exploratory two-author diagnostic (2026-10-26)

A private check used two sole-author medical research essays corpora, three articles per author. The evaluation withheld one whole article at a time and built each style profile from the other two. Five articles came from the same journal; one came from another journal. The seven-feature distance identified the closer author profile for **4 of 6** withheld articles. A simple sentence/paragraph-length comparator also identified **4 of 6**. Both same-journal withheld articles for one author were closer to the other author's profile in an additional one-article reference check. These are tiny, correlated, editorial-style samples and not a general accuracy estimate, but they directly fail to demonstrate author-specific style capture. The underlying papers and identities remain outside this public repository.

**Release judgment:** the rewrite tools remain experimental. A lower diagnostic distance or a plausible-looking draft must not be described as author-like. Do not tune on these six withheld articles and call the same cases independent validation. Use new authors and papers, topic/journal controls, actual LLM rewrites of identical content, and blinded human judgments before raising the quality claim.

### Follow-up feature ablations on the same six articles

The original seven-feature and length-only distances each selected the correct profile in 4/6 cases. Adding question and three rhetorical cue rates to the seven features also gave 4/6. A fixed list of 16 function words chosen in this exploratory iteration gave 6/6 alone and 6/6 when combined with the other features. Dropping one function-word feature at a time gave 6/6 for 15 choices and 5/6 when `the` was removed; dropping `the`, `of`, and `to` together gave 5/6. These are **post-inspection exploratory analyses of the same six cases**, so 6/6 is not an independent accuracy estimate. The heavy role of `the` could reflect subject matter or editorial differences rather than individual voice. A same-journal, single-reference-per-author comparison gave 4/4 on the four eligible held-out articles, but uses only one reference article per author and fails the product's two-development-family gate; do not report it as product performance.

For the same synthetic source draft, after adding rhetorical cues, both authors' plans still shared 3 of their 4 highest-priority instructions. The instructions remain insufficiently distinctive. There was no actual GPT/Claude generation in this diagnostic and no blinded likeness or factual review. The public repository stores only these anonymous counts; article identities and texts remain in private local evaluation storage.
